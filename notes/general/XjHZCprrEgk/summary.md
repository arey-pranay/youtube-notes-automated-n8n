# Database Choices for High-Scale Applications

## TL;DR
Choosing the right database for a high-scale application involves understanding access patterns, consistency needs, and scalability requirements. Netflix uses Cassandra for its write-heavy, globally distributed needs, Instagram leverages PostgreSQL for its relational data and flexible queries, and Twitter relies on Redis for its read-heavy, low-latency timeline serving. Each choice reflects a trade-off, prioritizing specific functionalities over others to meet unique application demands.

## Key Takeaways
- Netflix uses Cassandra for its write-heavy, globally distributed nature, handling 3M writes/sec.
- Instagram uses PostgreSQL, employing techniques like read replicas and partitioning to serve over 1 billion users with a read-heavy workload.
- Twitter uses Redis as an in-memory cache for its timeline service, achieving sub-millisecond latency by pre-computing timelines.
- The core principle of database selection is matching the access patterns and trade-offs to the right tool, not simply choosing the "best" database.
- Understanding data, queries, and trade-offs is crucial for effective system design.
