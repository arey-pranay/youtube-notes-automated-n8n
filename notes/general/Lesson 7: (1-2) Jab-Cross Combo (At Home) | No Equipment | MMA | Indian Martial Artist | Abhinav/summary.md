## Core Concepts Explained

### Jab
The jab is the lead punch in boxing, thrown with the hand that is forward in the boxer's stance. It is typically a straight punch, thrown quickly and with less power than the cross. The jab is used for a variety of purposes:
- **Measuring distance:** To gauge the opponent's range and reaction.
- **Setting up power punches:** To disrupt the opponent's guard or create an opening for a cross or hook.
- **Keeping the opponent at bay:** To maintain distance and control the pace of the fight.
- **Scoring points:** A quick jab can score points without overcommitting.

The execution involves extending the lead arm straight out, rotating the fist so the knuckles hit the target, and snapping the arm back to the guard position. Proper weight transfer and hip rotation are minimal for a jab, emphasizing speed and accuracy.

### Cross
The cross is a powerful, straight punch thrown with the rear hand. It is typically thrown after a jab or as a counter-punch. The cross involves a more significant rotation of the hips and torso, transferring weight from the back foot to the front foot, generating more power.

Execution involves:
1. **Stance:** Maintaining a stable, balanced stance.
2. **Rotation:** Rotating the hips and torso, bringing the rear shoulder forward.
3. **Extension:** Extending the rear arm straight towards the target, rotating the fist on impact.
4. **Recovery:** Snapping the arm back to the guard position quickly.

The cross is a primary power punch, used to damage the opponent or create significant openings.

### Boxing Combinations
Boxing combinations are sequences of punches thrown in rapid succession, often with coordinated footwork. They are crucial for overwhelming an opponent, creating openings, and maintaining offensive pressure. The effectiveness of a combination relies on:
- **Rhythm and timing:** The punches should flow smoothly from one to the next.
- **Power and speed:** Combining powerful punches with quick jabs.
- **Footwork:** Using foot movement to maintain balance, generate power, and create angles.
- **Defense:** Returning to a defensive guard after each punch.

Common combinations include:
- **Jab-Cross (1-2):** The most fundamental combination, using the lead hand for a quick jab followed by the rear hand for a powerful cross.
- **Jab-Jab-Cross (1-1-2):** Two quick jabs to set up a powerful cross.
- **Cross-Jab-Cross (2-1-2):** A rear hand cross, followed by a jab, and then another cross.
- **Jab-Cross-Jab (1-2-1):** A jab, a cross, and then another jab to re-establish distance or set up another attack.
