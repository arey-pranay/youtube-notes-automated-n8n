## Core Concepts Explained

### Geopolitical Escalation
Geopolitical escalation refers to the process by which tensions and conflicts between states or international actors increase in severity and scope. This can involve a series of actions and reactions, where each side's moves are perceived as threatening by the other, leading to a cycle of rising hostility. Factors contributing to geopolitical escalation include military buildup, diplomatic breakdowns, economic sanctions, and the use of propaganda. In the context of India and Pakistan, the acquisition of advanced military technology, border disputes, and historical animosities are key drivers of potential escalation. The risk of escalation is particularly concerning given that both nations possess nuclear weapons, raising the specter of a nuclear conflict.

### Stealth Fighter Jets
Stealth fighter jets are advanced military aircraft designed to evade detection by radar and other enemy surveillance systems. Their design incorporates specialized materials, shapes, and coatings that reduce their radar cross-section and infrared signature, making them difficult to track and target. These aircraft are equipped with sophisticated avionics, electronic warfare capabilities, and advanced weaponry, allowing them to penetrate enemy airspace, conduct reconnaissance, and engage targets with a high degree of survivability. The sale of such technology, like China's J-35AE to Pakistan, can significantly alter the military balance in a region and increase the potential for conflict.

### Nuclear Deterrence
Nuclear deterrence is a military strategy and doctrine that aims to prevent the use of nuclear weapons by threatening retaliation with nuclear weapons. The core principle is that any state considering a nuclear attack would face unacceptable consequences from a retaliatory strike, thus deterring them from initiating hostilities. This concept is often referred to as "mutually assured destruction" (MAD). In the context of India and Pakistan, both being nuclear-armed states, nuclear deterrence plays a crucial role in maintaining a fragile peace, as the potential for catastrophic retaliation discourages direct large-scale conflict. However, miscalculations, escalation from conventional conflict, or the proliferation of nuclear technology can undermine this deterrence.
